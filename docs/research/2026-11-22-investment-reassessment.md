## Check: what the CloneSeq evidence now establishes

The [content result](2026-11-22-cloneseq-content-result.md) and its [independent review](2026-11-22-cloneseq-content-independent-review.md) support 23 of 24 rostered patients with at least one exact SV/SNV sample-time key and 149 distinct shared keys. The separate key-only check linked all 12 author-listed WGS sample identifiers to S2 and both count sheets, across six patients. These are availability findings. The 149 keys are repeated measurements; the 12 WGS samples are not 12 independent patients. A common key does not establish a common clone, correct marker assignment, reference truth or equal assay effort. The reviewer identified an incomplete S10-key reporting issue; a separate worker is correcting the reporting. This decision does not depend on treating that correction as complete.

[Kabeer et al., cfClone v4](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.29.741620v4) report that **three of the six original CloneSeq-SV patients with WGS** had clone sets that CloneSeq-SV could distinguish with both SV and SNV markers in their analysis. Those three patients are the relevant independent patient units for that reported within-cohort comparison; additional markers and visits do not create more patients. This is not a claim that only three of all 24 patients could ever be evaluated by any other reference, assay or design. Nor is it proof that SVs or SNVs fail biologically in the remaining patients.

The same preprint already compares cfClone, SV-derived and SNV-derived estimates in this reused subset. Its three additional Vancouver patients provide a separate cfClone application, not an established independent SV-versus-SNV clone comparison. The preprint and [Williams et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12629990/) leave open a carefully budgeted incremental-value question, but they occupy generic claims about tracking clones from SVs, comparing trajectories, and using WGS signals as a reference. Replicating those comparisons on the same patients is development evidence, not external validation.

The [anonymous Synapse receipt](../../results/cloneseq_synapse_metadata_20261122_run01/receipt.json) records successful project/archive metadata requests and HTTP 403 for both the file-handle and download-reference requests. Authenticated access was not tested. This does not establish that the archive is permanently inaccessible, what its legal terms are, or what it contains. Even if an authorized download succeeds, it would not by itself add independent patients, establish clone truth, or make the assay budgets comparable.

## Check: reference, budget and selection

The original paper uses tissue-derived single-cell copy-number clone profiles for clone definitions. It uses uncorrected raw SV reads and duplex-consensus SNV sequences for clone-frequency estimates. It also uses copy-number calibration, normalization and an SV-based low-tumor-fraction exclusion. Those are reported methods, not interchangeable read units or an automatic fair-budget experiment. Preserve appropriate error correction in both arms; do not weaken the SNV baseline to equalize its processing. [Williams methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12629990/).

WGS read-depth and B-allele-frequency signals provide a separate molecular view, but share the biological sample and tissue-derived clone model. The paper also plots WGS predictions based on CloneSeq-SV fractions. Its WGS-similarity comparison is therefore useful **conditional validation**, not independent truth for every clone or proof of incremental benefit over SNVs. The [prior independent review](2026-11-22-cloneseq-content-independent-review.md) further notes that the pinned figure code joins clone-frequency records before filtering WGS comparison rows; the effect of that selection was not measured. cfClone adds a distinct inference model, not an independently observed ground truth for those same patients.

A fair question must state its budget. Removing SV features from an already purchased joint panel can assess a conditional information difference **given that joint assay**. It cannot establish the performance of a separately optimized SNV-only assay at the same cost. A resource-allocation claim needs defensible accounting for plasma input, targeted marker and probe design, unique molecules or sequencing families, total sequencing, error controls and relevant laboratory work. Marker count is a useful sensitivity axis, not a universal substitute for matching total cost or molecules. Raw SV reads are not independent molecules, and normalized clone frequencies cannot be downsampled as reads.

The literature update's proposed restriction to clones identifiable by both marker classes is a useful **conditional sensitivity analysis**, but it cannot be the sole practical-use denominator. It excludes marker scarcity and abstention—the very conditions under which one design might add or lose value. A later design should start with all prespecified eligible patients and observable clone contrasts, retain failed or unmeasured markers and no-calls, and report common-identifiability results separately. Do not select cases on favorable agreement, SV-derived tumor fraction, a future recurrence event, or a published discrepancy. Freeze decision-time inputs and use later measurements only as appropriately blinded evaluation evidence. A patient-level interval or resampling scheme must use patients as clusters; resampling variants or a bootstrap over three patients does not create cohort power.

## Check: what would reopen CloneSeq

Reopen a **prospective comparative design** when the following are concretely demonstrated for the claim being made, with cost and missingness stated:

1. **Accessible measurements.** A permitted source supplies the same-specimen marker counts, processing and error-control information, candidate clone/marker maps, exclusions, and the information needed for the chosen budget. The anonymous 403 is a reason to mark access unresolved, not proof that authenticated access will fail. A successful login or archive listing alone is not a scientific go decision.
2. **An endpoint-appropriate reference.** Establish which clone contrasts a separate molecular measurement can distinguish, its provenance and uncertainty, and whether it shares the model or input with either arm. For an analytical question, independently characterized mixtures or orthogonal measurements may be appropriate; a perfect clinical outcome label is not a universal requirement. A claim about a relapse-seeding lineage needs an appropriate independent lineage assessment, with incomplete or multiple lineages allowed as uncertain.
3. **A credible independent evaluation.** Identify a patient- or mixture-disjoint evaluation source beyond the reused six-patient WGS subset, with a prespecified eligibility flow and enough independent units for uncertainty around a meaningful effect. The number must follow the chosen endpoint, error target and useful effect size; three patients are a severe precision limit, not an arbitrary universal minimum. Technical repeats and related samples must not be relabeled as independent patients or mixtures.
4. **A distinct comparison and strong baselines.** Freeze SNV-only versus SNV-plus-SV as the primary added-information comparison, with a strong multi-marker SNV method and each arm's legitimate error processing. Specify material, sequencing and design cost; model missingness and abstention; and keep any SV-only or common-identifiable-clone comparison secondary. An ECLIPSE-like baseline is relevant where its assay and inputs are compatible, not automatically runnable on these ovarian tables. [TRACERx/ECLIPSE primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7614605/).
5. **A contribution beyond prior art.** State before comparative outcomes what decision the new result could change—for example, reliable clone assignment or abstention at a defined input and error budget—and compare against CloneSeq-SV, cfClone and modern multi-marker SNV analysis within their valid scopes. A new cancer label, high correlation, extra time points or lower raw background alone is insufficient. Independent, well-powered negative evidence could also be valuable if it rules out a prespecified useful gain; an underpowered non-result cannot.

These are conditions for the stated generalizable comparison, not claims that a single dataset must contain every possible label or that every useful molecular audit needs a clinical outcome. A limited mechanistic or analytical result can be valuable under a narrower, honest claim.

### What a cheap CloneSeq diagnostic could establish

If a legitimate authenticated route or a specifically identified public source is available, a bounded **metadata and methods check** could determine whether the required WGS profiles, clone mappings, count units, molecule-family information, error controls and use conditions exist and can be linked. It could document the patient/clone/timepoint eligibility flow and estimate the maximum independent sample count under a predeclared reference. The check should avoid clinical outcome values and comparative scoring. A positive result would justify a design and precision assessment, not a campaign.

It cannot infer equal input or cost from the workbook's shared keys; make cfClone's estimates independent truth; increase the number of independent patients by resampling markers; show superiority or safety; or turn a negative/403/missing asset into absence of biology. Repeat anonymous requests or another filename inventory have little value unless they resolve a named missing input or a changed access condition. An authenticated metadata result would resolve only access to the tested object.

## Check: generic SV–RNA alternative

The fixed 56 names in the [paired review](2026-11-06-paired-scientific-review.md) are matching RNA and HGSVC2 header names, not 56 confirmed callable alleles, matched aliquots or informative RNA molecules. They are a feasible source frame, not evidence for a new mechanism. [Ebert et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8026704/) already studied SV expression and splicing QTLs in 427 donors; [Lu et al.'s preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13345107/) evaluates pangenome-based expression mapping in 430 samples and includes a 201-sample long-read RNA cohort. Generic SV–expression association, a different RNA platform, or another overlap count is weak novelty.

A credible residual would be a specified, sequence-resolved allele and an allele-linked transcript junction or interpretation problem that strong existing sequence-aware and linked-SNV/indel baselines do not explain. It would need callable carriers and non-carriers, exact allele/reference reconciliation, independent molecule-level RNA support and phase where the claim requires them, and controls for mapping, linked variants, population structure and transcript-catalogue leakage. A count matrix or BED alone does not prove an insertion and junction occur on the same molecule. If allele-informative reads or a useful denominator are missing, report non-evaluability. The chromosome-22 pilot is a bounded candidate screen, not a representative genome-wide verdict or an automatic successor to CloneSeq.

## Check: open hard-region benchmark alternative

The [SMaHT MIMS benchmark](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12458932/) already uses a physical mixture of six HapMap donor cell lines, assembly-derived truth and multiple sequencing-center/platform runs. It evaluates 12 strategies, low-allele-fraction detection and difficult sequence contexts. Its [official resource](https://github.com/BCM-HGSC/SMaHT_MIMS) distinguishes collapsed and allele-resolved benchmark representations and describes joint germline/somatic matching. The centers and platforms are technical replicates of the same pool, not independent mixtures. A fresh caller ranking, low-fraction coverage curve or claim that repetitive regions are difficult would largely repeat prior work. An open resource is not proof that every exact query file, score, mask or truth allele is accessible and appropriate.

The reviewed [COLO829 donor-specific assembly study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.09.033) already demonstrates an important reference-coverage effect. A junction outside a benchmark's declared reference or callable territory is out of scope, not automatically a false negative. A nearby coordinate is not proof of the same allele; sample passage, clone, reference sequence, event representation and truth support matter. Nor is a component-only call a wrong phased path when the caller made no path assertion. [Verix](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag803) already covers complex-event breakpoint completeness and fragmentation, while [vcfdist](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-024-03394-5) covers phased local sequence comparison. The [complex-path review](2026-11-03-complex-path-prior-art.md) describes a narrower possible adjacency/path question, not a demonstrated gap.

A publication-worthy residual could be an independently supported, decision-relevant allele or explicitly reported derivative-path discrepancy **inside a declared evaluable domain**, or a generalizable way to distinguish a specific minority allele from a near-identical background with calibrated abstention. It must add information beyond native caller evidence, existing hard-allele/joint matching, Verix and applicable sequence-level comparators. A single fixed case can motivate investigation; it cannot establish prevalence or broad method superiority. Independent allele/locus groups or mixtures, held-out evaluation, defensible truth, explicit unknowns and uncertainty would be needed for the corresponding general claim. Clinical labels are not mandatory for a sequence-inference claim.

### The recommended cheap diagnostic

Use the three junctions already named by the existing review as a **fixed development set**, retaining every one. For each, use public source descriptions or small released manifests to record: the exact event and allele representation; tumor/normal sample and passage; reference and benchmark version; callable territory and event eligibility; available explicit caller output; and whether the original paper already resolves the alleged discrepancy. Match actual sequence and event identity rather than coordinates alone. Mark each comparison **evaluable**, **outside declared scope**, or **unresolved**, with the named missing input and cost to obtain it.

No observed caller error is required before this check, and no two-of-three success rule is warranted. A case that is only outside the reference, already explained by the original paper, or not tied to a comparable allele does not establish a new defect. One supported residual may justify a separately designed native-comparator test; the three published cases are selected development examples, not a held-out population sample. A negative or unresolved crosswalk closes only this finite candidate, not all hard-region or somatic-SV research. Larger downloads and compute remain possible when a concrete question justifies them.
