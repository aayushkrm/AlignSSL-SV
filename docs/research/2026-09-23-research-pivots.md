# Research pivots for AlignSSL-SV

**Literature cutoff:** 2026-09-23. This is an independent assessment of research directions, not an endorsement of the earlier SSL plan. DeepSV is not used to motivate any direction; if retained in a future experiment, it should be labeled a historical baseline only.

## Decision summary

The best current thesis to test is **whether SV errors and missed alleles disproportionately persist in difficult sequence contexts and held-out haplotypes, even after modern caller ensembles and pangenome-aware resources are used**. It is potentially publishable as a methods study only if the gap is demonstrated with independent, multi-sample truth and is not an artifact of reference build, truth set, matcher, or platform. The most important first measurement is the *candidate-recall ceiling*: filtering cannot recover an allele absent from its input candidates.

Do not start with a new representation-learning model or a large data download. First use existing public callsets and truth resources to measure (i) candidate completeness, (ii) error by prespecified difficult-region strata, and (iii) whether conclusions survive alternate truth sets and matchers. Proceed to a new method only if a donor-held-out, truth-aware diagnostic leaves a material, reproducible gap.

| Direction | Priority | Novelty risk | Cheapest falsifier |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Difficult-region and held-out-population generalization | **P1** | Medium-high: strong evidence of a hard-region problem, but not yet a new solution | Re-score existing calls against independent assembly truth by donor, LCR/mappability, allele class, and population/haplotype stratum |
| Candidate generation beyond caller/catalog pools | **P2** | High: filtering is crowded; the missing-candidate ceiling is a sharper opening | Measure truth-in-union recall before fitting/scoring anything |
| Realistic call-set evaluation | **P2, enabling** | High for another caller leaderboard; medium for a new multi-truth, multi-donor protocol | Test rank/metric stability across truth set, reference, and matcher on an existing benchmark, then replicate across unrelated donors |
| Breakpoint refinement and genotyping | **P3** | High for generic refinement; medium for a narrow complex/repetitive-allele problem | Compare current caller genotypes/refinement with orthogonal haplotype truth at a small, stratified locus set |
| Representation learning | **P4; conditional only** | Very high absent external transfer gains | Donor-held-out comparison against strong tree-based and caller-native baselines using the same inputs and label budget |

## Evidence conventions

**Verified** means the cited primary paper, preprint, official documentation, or official repository was checked for the stated fact. Preprints are explicitly labeled and should not be treated as peer-reviewed evidence. **Inference** marks a proposed gap, expected value, feasibility estimate, or priority judgment; it is a research hypothesis, not a literature fact. Compute categories below are planning judgments, not measured runtimes.

## 1. Difficult-region and held-out-population generalization — P1

**Closest prior work — verified.** The 2025 GIAB low-complexity-region study reports that 35.4 Mb (about 1.2% of GRCh38) contains 69.1% of HG002 confident SVs, and that 77.3–91.3% of long-read caller errors in its analysis fall in low-complexity regions. It also documents how a newer allele-resolved HG002 truth set changes conclusions, and discusses limitations of the single-sample analysis. [Peer-reviewed study, GigaScience](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giaf154). Separately, Audano and Beck report that small polymorphisms can shift read-based SV breakpoints and that the shifts vary among phased haplotypes, establishing a concrete route by which reference/haplotype mismatch can bias breakpoint placement. [Peer-reviewed study, Genome Research](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.278203.123).

Population-scale resources are improving quickly: the [HPRC 2023 draft pangenome](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05896-x) established graph-based population representation as a serious comparator, while the [official HPRC data-release documentation](https://humanpangenome.org/hprc-data-release-2/) describes a much larger, updated resource. A 2026 [HPRC2 preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.21.739710) reports 460 haplotypes/230 samples, but also documents residual assembly uncertainty in difficult regions; pangenome membership therefore must not be equated with perfect truth. A 2026 [population SV resource preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.21.26361050) integrates near-T2T genomes and long-read samples at population scale, making it a close comparator/resource rather than an unoccupied area.

**Gap and novelty risk — inference.** The opening is not simply “SV callers fail in repeats” or “add ancestry.” Those are known. The potentially new question is whether a method has a *reproducible, donor-held-out error or recall deficit* in hard sequence contexts and underrepresented haplotypes, after controlling for caller, sequencing platform, reference, truth-set ascertainment, and candidate availability. Then test a targeted remedy—such as haplotype-aware local evidence or uncertainty-aware abstention—against existing pangenome/caller pipelines. Risk is medium-high: HG002 LCR limitations and general haplotype effects are known, so a single-sample or post-hoc subgroup report is unlikely to establish novelty.

**Why it matters — inference.** Aggregate genome-wide F1 can hide clinically and biologically consequential loci where mapping ambiguity, repeat structure, or reference divergence concentrates errors. A robust method that generalizes across donors and those loci is more valuable than a small average-score gain on random held-out variants.

**Cheapest falsification diagnostic.** Without training a model, take existing callsets and independent assembly-resolved truth for at least one benchmark donor and one unrelated/held-out donor if available. Freeze the reference and matcher; report per-donor precision/recall and genotype concordance by SV class/size, LCR or other prespecified hard-region mask, mappability, and haplotype novelty. Bootstrap at donor/locus units, not reads or individual calls. If the apparent deficit disappears under a second truth set or is explained by candidate absence, route it to direction 2 or 4; if no repeatable hard-region or held-out-donor gap remains, stop this thesis.

**Data and compute.** A useful pilot can start from public VCFs, truth VCFs/assemblies, and region masks; storage and compute are low-to-moderate. A credible multi-donor study needs raw or consistently processed long-read evidence, independent assemblies/orthogonal truth, reference/build provenance, and enough unrelated samples in each evaluated stratum. Re-aligning/calling whole genomes raises compute and storage substantially. Platinum Pedigree supplies valuable multi-generation truth but its relatives are not independent ancestry cohorts: [official datasets](https://github.com/Platinum-Pedigree-Consortium/Platinum-Pedigree-Datasets), [peer-reviewed resource paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02750-y). HPRC2 is promising but is a preprint and has documented difficult-region assembly gaps.

**Rigorous baselines.** Run current caller(s) on matched input/reference; caller union/ensemble; the best relevant linear-reference and pangenome-aware pipelines; and a simple local-assembly or haplotype-aware comparator where feasible. Use the same truth versions, masks, sample splits, and matching policy. Report candidate recall separately from conditional caller/filter recall, plus precision, genotype concordance, calibration/abstention, and donor-level uncertainty. Do not call each related pedigree member an independent replicate.

**Likely failure mode.** “Ancestry effect” may actually be platform, coverage, reference, or truth-set confounding; the hard-region mask may exclude precisely the unresolvable loci; and too few unrelated donors can make subgroup uncertainty enormous. Avoid essentializing ancestry: define held-out donors/haplotypes and measurable sequence/mapping context, and state which population labels are descriptive rather than causal.

## 2. Candidate generation beyond caller/catalog pools — P2

**Closest prior work — verified.** Dicast is a 2026 peer-reviewed XGBoost filter using more than 100 alignment/context features, trained on curated multi-technology callsets and applied to candidates from callers or population resources. Its design and reported results are candidate-dependent: filtering can rank, suppress, or rescue candidates it receives, but it does not establish recall for alleles absent from that candidate pool. [Peer-reviewed Genome Biology article](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-026-04280-y), [official code](https://github.com/burgshrimps/dicast). The field also has a 2026 [preprint comparing SV filtering models](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.01.27.702059) and earlier supervised/contrastive filtering such as [CSV-Filter (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae539). A 2026 short-read benchmark of 14 callers further shows that caller choice and ensembling remain active baselines, not a solved comparison: [peer-reviewed Genome Biology benchmark](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-026-04219-3).

**Gap and novelty risk — inference.** A defensible opening is *new-allele/candidate recall outside the union of contemporary callers and catalogs*, especially for insertions or repeat-associated alleles, followed by evidence-based refinement. A higher F1 on a filtered, caller-generated candidate list is not candidate-generation novelty. Risk is high because current callers, ensembles, population catalogs, and ML filters already cover much of the apparent space; a paper needs to show that a materially useful set of true alleles is systematically absent and that the proposed discovery mechanism finds them without simply overcalling noise.

**Why it matters — inference.** Candidate generation sets a hard upper bound on every downstream filter and genotype model. If this ceiling is low in the scientifically important strata, optimizing a classifier on existing calls cannot solve the main problem.

**Cheapest falsification diagnostic.** Before building a generator, compute **truth-in-candidate-union recall** for each truth allele class and prespecified region stratum: match orthogonal assembly/benchmark alleles against each caller, the caller union, and the relevant catalog. Report the denominator and matching sensitivity. Repeat with an alternate truth/matcher where possible. If the union already recovers the target alleles at the predeclared useful level—or the “missing” set vanishes under representation harmonization—do not build a candidate generator. If a gap remains, manually inspect a small blinded sample of missed truth loci to determine whether raw reads contain discoverable evidence.

**Data and compute.** The first diagnostic uses existing VCFs and truth with low compute. Developing discovery beyond callers requires raw reads, independent haplotype/assembly truth, and likely local assembly or read-level evidence; the full cohort can be high-compute. Dicast’s curated training data are described by the paper, but the article indicates access constraints for some source samples; do not make the project’s reproducibility depend on inaccessible data. [Dicast article](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-026-04280-y).

**Rigorous baselines.** Every relevant individual caller; a union and a documented ensemble; the appropriate population catalog/pangenome genotype pipeline; and Dicast only on a compatible short-read task and with a reproducible model/data path. For long-read inputs, compare contemporary callers designed for that technology rather than importing a short-read filter as if it were equivalent. Report truth-in-pool recall, precision after discovery, sequence/allele-level matches, and downstream genotype accuracy separately.

**Likely failure mode.** The “novel” candidates are duplicates or alternate representations of known catalog alleles, or the truth source is itself caller-derived and misses the same variant classes. Another risk is measuring an oracle candidate pool with reads/candidates from the test donor, which leaks labels or prior knowledge into discovery. Use donor-held-out evaluation, freeze catalog versions, and document all candidate sources.

## 3. Realistic call-set evaluation — P2, enabling direction

**Closest prior work — verified.** Evaluation sensitivity is now directly studied. A 2025 preprint reports that SV caller rankings change across reference builds, truth sets, genomic regions, and normalization/matching choices: [Denti et al. preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.08.28.672546). The 2025 peer-reviewed [ASVBM](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2025.06.045) addresses local joint matching and multi-allelic/adjacent callsets, with an [official implementation](https://github.com/zhuxiao/asvbm). The 2025 GIAB LCR work shows how a newer allele-resolved truth can alter benchmark conclusions ([GigaScience](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giaf154)); a 2026 benchmark compares 14 short-read callers across samples ([Genome Biology](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-026-04219-3)). These are close prior work, so “we benchmarked more callers” is a weak novelty claim.

**Gap and novelty risk — inference.** A publication-worthy evaluation contribution would need a materially broader and more realistic unit of evidence: unrelated donors, difficult-region and haplotype strata, multiple independent truths where available, explicit reference/matcher sensitivity, genotype and inheritance/orthogonal validation, and raw reproducible artifacts. It should answer a decision question (which methods fail on which alleles and why), not just produce another leaderboard. Risk is high as a standalone benchmark unless there is new independent data, an evaluation method, or a consequential conclusion that existing studies could not establish.

**Why it matters — inference.** SV precision/recall depends on what counts as truth and whether differently represented but equivalent alleles are matched. An evaluation that makes those choices visible helps prevent fragile method rankings and overclaimed gains.

**Cheapest falsification diagnostic.** Re-evaluate an already available multi-caller sample across two independent truth versions (for example, the applicable GIAB versions), at least two reference/matching configurations, and a prespecified difficult-region mask. Quantify rank correlation and per-method deltas. This is a replication/triage exercise, not by itself a publication. If conclusions are stable, the proposed benchmark gap is smaller; if unstable, test the phenomenon in unrelated donors and with orthogonal genotype/inheritance evidence before claiming a new evaluation contribution.

**Data and compute.** Re-scoring existing calls is low-compute and often needs only callsets, benchmark masks, and exact version metadata. Producing independent truth or rerunning many callers is moderate-to-high compute. Public starting points include [GIAB benchmark resources](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/genome-bottle) and the [Platinum Pedigree datasets](https://github.com/Platinum-Pedigree-Consortium/Platinum-Pedigree-Datasets); access, license, sample relationships, truth scope, and assembly versions must be checked before treating them as a common test set.

**Rigorous baselines.** At least two contemporary callers and their union/ensemble on identical reads/build; more than one applicable truth resource/version; ordinary and sequence-aware matching (including ASVBM where appropriate); genotype and, for family data, Mendelian concordance. Report precision/recall, genotype accuracy, candidate ceiling, calibration where a score exists, uncertainty, and sensitivity to truth/matcher/build. Pre-register a primary metric and avoid tuning thresholds on the final test cohort.

**Likely failure mode.** A large factorial benchmark creates many opportunities to cherry-pick a favorable mask, truth, threshold, or caller subset. Truth sets are not independent if derived from the same sequencing/call pipelines, and difficult-region exclusions can make metrics look better by deleting hard biology. Keep one untouched final cohort, distinguish truth from high-confidence masks, and disclose all tested configurations and exclusions.

## 4. Breakpoint refinement and genotyping — P3

**Closest prior work — verified.** FocalSV performs long-read, haplotype-aware local assembly and SV detection/refinement/genotyping at target or automatically selected regions, across multiple long-read technologies: [peer-reviewed Genome Research paper](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.280282.124), [official code](https://github.com/maiziezhoulab/FocalSV). Cue provides image-based discovery/genotyping for several large SV classes: [peer-reviewed Nature Methods paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01799-x), [official code](https://github.com/PopicLab/cue). NPSV-deep is a simulation-conditioned short-read genotyper for DEL/INS: [peer-reviewed paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10955255/), [official code](https://github.com/mlinderm/npsv2). Population-scale breakpoint integration is also emerging in the 2026 [BoostSV resource preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.21.26361050). Generic “use local assembly to refine SVs” or “add a neural genotyper” is therefore not an open claim by itself.

**Gap and novelty risk — inference.** A narrower opening may remain for complex, multiallelic, repeat-associated alleles where breakpoint coordinates alone are inadequate: jointly resolve allele sequence, haplotype, and genotype, and report uncertainty when reads cannot distinguish alternatives. Show that the target failure is not already fixed by FocalSV, a pangenome-aware method, or the caller’s native genotype. Novelty risk is high unless the variant class, input modality, and failure mechanism are sharply specified.

**Why it matters — inference.** Small breakpoint shifts or normalization differences can turn a biologically equivalent allele into an apparent false positive/negative; ambiguous genotype calls also make population association and clinical interpretation unreliable.

**Cheapest falsification diagnostic.** Select a small, prespecified set of loci stratified by simple versus complex/repetitive structure and existing confidence. Compare original caller breakpoints/genotypes with orthogonal assembly/haplotype truth and sequence-aware matching. Run the closest compatible existing refiner/genotyper on exactly those loci. If its residual errors are rare, representation-only, or unresolvable from available reads, stop before training a new model.

**Data and compute.** Needs raw reads or BAM/CRAM plus allele-resolved assemblies/truth; local read extraction and assembly is moderate for a locus pilot, but genome-wide multi-donor long-read processing can be high-compute. A small pilot can avoid new sequencing/downloads if compatible public evidence already exists.

**Rigorous baselines.** Native caller breakpoint and genotype; the best compatible local-assembly/refinement method (including FocalSV for its scope); NPSV-deep only for supported short-read DEL/INS tasks; and pangenome/sequence-aware matching. Keep platform and variant-class scope matched. Measure breakpoint distance only alongside allele-sequence equivalence, genotype concordance, Mendelian consistency where valid, and calibrated no-call/uncertainty behavior.

**Likely failure mode.** A nominal breakpoint “improvement” is only a different representation of the same allele, while genotype truth is itself ambiguous. Another common failure is comparing specialized tools outside their supported SV classes or technology and interpreting the resulting gap as method superiority.

## 5. Representation learning — P4, conditional rather than the objective

**Closest prior work — verified.** CSV-Filter already explored contrastive/self-supervised representation learning for SV filtering: [peer-reviewed Bioinformatics paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae539), [official repository](https://github.com/xzyschumacher/CSV-Filter). The 2026 [SV-filtering comparison preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.01.27.702059) compares engineered-feature/tree and deep approaches and reports similar peak F1 across several approaches on its evaluated samples. Dicast’s 2026 peer-reviewed XGBoost approach is another strong non-neural filter ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-026-04280-y), [code](https://github.com/burgshrimps/dicast)). These results make “SSL improves SV filtering” an especially risky generic thesis.

**Gap and novelty risk — inference.** Representation learning is justified only if it solves a diagnosed transfer problem—for example, held-out-donor, held-out-platform, or hard-region generalization at the same candidate pool and label budget—where simpler features and modern supervised baselines demonstrably fail. Novelty risk is very high if the evidence is only random call-level splits or average F1 on one donor/caller.

**Why it matters — inference.** A representation that genuinely transfers across donors and platforms could reduce dependence on costly labels, but that benefit is not established merely by pretraining or by a higher in-domain score.

**Cheapest falsification diagnostic.** On a fixed, donor-held-out split and identical inputs, compare a regularized logistic model, a strong random-forest/gradient-boosted-tree baseline, caller-native filters, and a small neural model. Keep the label budget and tuning budget equal; include a within-donor random split only as a leakage diagnostic, never as the primary result. If the representation does not deliver a prespecified practical gain in recall at fixed precision, calibration, or transfer on the held-out donor—and uncertainty excludes a useful effect—do not scale pretraining.

**Data and compute.** Once candidate-level features are prepared, these baselines are low-to-moderate compute. Large read-level pretraining or multi-platform representation learning can be high GPU/storage cost and should be gated on the diagnostic. The 2026 comparison preprint uses a limited donor/caller scope; generalization beyond that scope remains an inference to test, not a demonstrated conclusion.

**Rigorous baselines.** Caller-native scores; engineered-feature RF/gradient boosting; Dicast when compatible and reproducible; CSV-Filter or another closest representation model; and a no-pretraining neural ablation. Hold out donors (and, where relevant, caller/platform) rather than random variants. Report candidate coverage, fixed-precision recall, calibration, subgroup behavior, donor-level uncertainty, and compute/label budgets.

**Likely failure mode.** Leakage through adjacent/same-locus variants, donor-specific read signatures, caller identity, or truth-generation artifacts produces impressive random-split metrics but no transfer. Model complexity and tuning consume resources without exceeding a strong tree baseline; F1 hides poor precision/calibration at the intended operating point.

## Cross-cutting experiment and data gates

1. **Start with a no-training audit.** Inventory available callsets, truth, assemblies, masks, sample relationships, licenses, versions, and checksums. Measure candidate recall and stratified errors before any new model or broad data acquisition.
2. **Freeze the split at the donor/haplotype level.** Keep a final test cohort untouched through candidate choice, feature engineering, and threshold selection. Random variant splits are not adequate evidence of population or donor generalization.
3. **Separate ceilings.** Report truth-in-candidate-pool recall, conditional classification/filter metrics, breakpoint/allele equivalence, and genotype accuracy as distinct quantities.
4. **Budget tuning and compute fairly.** Give each baseline the same training donors and comparable tuning budget; record tool versions, CPU/GPU hours, memory, and failed runs. For expensive reruns, pilot one representative sample and estimate the full cohort before allocating cluster resources.
5. **Use uncertainty at the biological sampling unit.** Resample donors or independent loci, not reads or thousands of correlated alleles; prespecify primary strata/endpoints and adjust or clearly label exploratory subgroup tests.
6. **Preserve reproducibility.** Keep raw commands/configs, input checksums, caller logs, intermediate candidate sets, and immutable result manifests. Do not overwrite prior results. Record scratch expiry and ensure required artifacts are copied to durable project storage before cleanup.
7. **Acquire new data only if the falsifier requires it.** Public resources are not interchangeable: some are preprints, some contain related individuals, and some truth sets have difficult-region gaps. First verify sample provenance, modality, access, truth scope, and compatibility; then download only the smallest panel that can distinguish the competing hypotheses.

## Source status and interpretation notes

- Peer-reviewed sources cited above include the Dicast and short-read benchmark articles (2026), the GIAB LCR analysis (2025), Platinum Pedigree (2025), ASVBM (2025), FocalSV (2025), CSV-Filter (2024), NPSV-deep (2024), Cue (2023), and HPRC (2023). DOI/publisher links are provided inline.
- Explicit preprints: the January 2026 SV filtering comparison, the Denti 2025 evaluation study, HPRC2 (July 2026), and the population SV resource (August 2026). Their results are promising primary evidence, not peer-reviewed consensus.
- Prior-work examples are selected to identify the closest novelty risks, not to claim an exhaustive systematic review. Priority rankings and proposed gaps are reasoned inferences. Before a manuscript claim, recheck publication status, benchmark versions, tool releases, and data access.
