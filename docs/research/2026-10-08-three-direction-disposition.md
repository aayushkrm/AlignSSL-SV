## Family gate needs a calibrated assay law

For zero detections from N independent callable haploid targets with perfect
ALT detection, the one-sided 95% bound is `1 - 0.05^(1/N)`.
At N=300 this is about 0.9936%. With known sensitivity s and negligible false
positives, the corresponding bound is `min(1, (1 - 0.05^(1/N))/s)`.
A passing 1% positive control is not proof of perfect detection, effective N,
or certainty about s. Reads/PCR copies are not independent gametic targets.
Sample timing, gamete recovery and selection also limit transmission claims.
No assay was run and no clinical recurrence estimate is made.

## Mitochondrial origin counterexample: no input search needed

Consider an ancestral cell carrying deletion junction J. Its descendants
split into two independently barcoded cell lineages. Later, a neutral mtDNA
variant a appears on a J-bearing molecule in the first lineage, and b appears
on a J-bearing molecule in the second. Observations are J+a and J+b, with
different external lineage labels, despite exactly ONE deletion origin.
Conversely, the same J can arise independently in two cells sharing the same
ancestral mtDNA haplotype, giving indistinguishable linked sequence features.

These are permitted histories, not observed biological events or a universal
impossibility proof. Their existence defeats the proposed interpretation:
better held-out clone prediction plus orthogonal deletion confirmation cannot
alone adjudicate repeated deletion origins. Additional temporal/event evidence
or defensible restrictions can change that conclusion. None is established here.
Do not follow the worker's suggested MitoTracer availability check under this
framing. Even a table with clone labels and linked deletion reads would not
by itself repair the mismatch between the label and scientific target.
