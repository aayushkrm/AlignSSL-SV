## Biological significance is real; generic novelty is not

[Handsaker et al., Cell](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674%2824%2901379-5)
was published online January 16, 2025, outside that window but essential prior
work. Repeat measurements come from barcoded HTT transcripts, paired to the
same nuclei's RNA profiles. They are not repeated DNA measurements of a
living cell. Native controls include UMI consensus, paired transcripts,
separate long/short libraries, and decoding unaligned reads. The pipeline
retains the largest UMI-level estimate per cell when several are available.
Regression includes donor, SPN subtype and library-size terms.
Raw reads are controlled access; cell-level length, expression and metadata
are described as open NeMO data. No archive contents were inspected here.

[Methods S1](https://www.cell.com/cms/10.1016/j.cell.2024.11.038/attachment/41584a12-efe4-46f6-a38e-468f0a25d177/mmc6.pdf)
explicitly treats PCR chimerism, size separation, alternative expression
models and expansion-model uncertainty. It compares candidate hinges rather
than treating 150 as an externally fixed biological constant. The model's
past-neuron-loss input is estimated, not a tracked cell-fate series.
Do not propose these existing controls as new work or infer that RNA-derived
length measurements invalidate the study.

[Kay et al., Neuron](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(26)00640-9)
is marked online September 14, 2026. It directly studies loss of interruption
(LOI), DNA repeat distributions in brain/blood, sorted neuronal nuclei and
MSN counts. Its reported brain-versus-blood effect differs in direction.
This is affirmative overlap against a generic interruption-risk or tissue
transfer claim. Data are described as available on request, not an identified
open processed download. No authors were contacted or access requested.

[CAGinSTEM, Cell Reports](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247(25)01457-3)
has a retrieved publication date of December 23, 2025; complete publication
history was not verified. It uses edited human stem-cell clones, neuronal
differentiation, allele-resolved repeat sequence and phenotypic assays.
Removing/adding CAA interruptions already tests a consequential cis mechanism.
Plasmid/PCR comparisons and allele-specific amplification are native controls.
Its differentiation cultures include both post-mitotic and cycling cells;
bulk measurements are not single-cell mutation histories. PCR removes native
methylation information. The paper provides T-Rex code and names SRA
PRJNA1074134; no source inventory, sample joins or assay independence is verified.
These in vitro findings are not labels for clinical progression or neuronal
death in each human nucleus. Reanalysis cannot silently replace that estimand.

## Concrete candidate considered, not selected

Question: can repeat-distribution inference separate within-cell expansion
from assay distortion and changes in the sampled cell population well enough
to improve a fixed, consequential cis-interruption conclusion?

Why it matters: pooled length distributions can change through mutation,
sampling or population selection. These explanations need not imply the same
intervention effect. This is a proposed question, not a demonstrated error.
Native work already has strong distortion controls and functional interventions.
An extra caller, interruption feature, RNA-expression predictor or generic
length-to-phenotype correlation is not a distinct contribution.

One possible falsifier would fix an edited-clone contrast and assay/time points
before outcomes, then ask whether a native-supported measurement model yields
the same biological conclusion as a claimed new model under independently
measured technical controls. Stop if the new result is only standard mixture
fitting, depends on unmeasured selection/capture assumptions, or changes a
metric without changing a defensible biological inference. No such new model,
frozen contrast or independently adjudicated residual error is established here.
Cell-level transcript precision does not identify unobserved allele dropout;
bulk clone distributions do not by themselves identify cell-level transitions.
Unknown validation inputs are unresolved, not proof of their absence.
No dataset transfer, computational analysis or functional experiment follows.
