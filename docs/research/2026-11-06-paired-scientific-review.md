# Independent scientific investment review

**Date:** 2026-11-06. **Scope:** the paired-source packet in `5b4a447`, the later HGSVC2 and Vienna header checks, and a bounded challenge of the separate TRACERx scout. This is an investment review, not a second audit of the original 24-match parser. No genotype or expression values were inspected, no biological result was accepted, and no expensive job was run.

## Decision

| Direction | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Paired DNA/RNA | **Stop the broad SV–RNA/QTL proposal as an investment case.** Allow only the small falsifier below. | The sources establish useful names and headers, not a cohort of measured SV alleles or phased RNA outcomes. Ebert, HGSVC3, the RNA authors, and Lu already cover much of the generic claim. |
| TRACERx ctDNA | **No-go for an experiment or claim now; retain as a conditional question.** | CloneSeq-SV already compares clone-specific SV and SNV tracking. TRACERx's patient-level join, independent relapse labels, junction measurements, access and matched assay budget are not established. |

Neither decision says that SVs cannot affect RNA or that junctions cannot improve NSCLC tracking. A narrower, useful residual would need evidence that an existing strong baseline misses a decision-relevant outcome. No such residual has yet been demonstrated by these packets.

```text
60 RNA titles = 60 unique columns in the verified FLAIR count-file header

24 = 11 Vienna alignment-list names + 13 ONT-500 metadata names
56 = exact names in the checked HGSVC2 PanGenie VCF header
58 = 24 ∪ 56, using the separately audited 24-name crosswalk

Nine of the 11 Vienna names are in its checked phased-VCF header.
All nine are already among the 56 HGSVC2 names.
```

The 58 is a **mixed-evidence union**, not 58 verified paired genomes, 58 long-read genotypes, or 58 informative outcomes. The saved flags give 22 names in both the earlier 24 and HGSVC2, 34 in HGSVC2 only, two in the earlier set only, and two in neither. ## Closest prior art and the remaining question

| Primary source | What it already covers | Boundary for this proposal |
|---|---|---|
| [Ebert et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abf7117) | The authors used assembly-discovered SVs and PanGenie genotypes with LCL/GEUVADIS RNA to map both eQTLs and sQTLs in a combined 427-donor analysis. | This is especially close: the proposed HGSVC2 source is from the same Freeze 3 genotyping family. Exact donor and allele overlap with the 60-line RNA study is not yet proved. Reusing a donor with a new assay is not an independent donor replication. |
| [HGSVC3, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09140-6) | The authors aligned Iso-Seq from 12 LCL donors to donor-matched haplotype assemblies, examined isoforms in SV-containing genes, and assessed nearby gene expression. | Phased SVs plus long-read isoforms are established. Their reported associations are not automatically causal truth. The [author correction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09547-1) changes a figure-axis unit. |
| [Réal et al., 2026 preprint](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-4613444/v2) | The RNA authors describe these 60 LCLs, FLAIR/Bambu/StringTie quantification, and transcript–SNP QTL analysis. They report 87 significant transcript QTLs; these are author results, not newly validated here. Their methods describe small-variant genotypes and select lead SNPs. | Generic discovery of transcript associations in this exact RNA cohort is already occupied. This does not establish that every SV, allele-specific junction, or comparison in a different catalogue was tested. |
| [Lu et al., 2026 preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.01.735728) | The authors compare 1KG, PanGenie and graph-derived calls for eQTL discovery, fine-mapping and expression prediction in 430 AFGR LCL samples with short-read RNA. They also analyze an HPRC subset of 201 with PacBio Kinnex long-read RNA, using a pooled transcript catalogue and gene-level eQTLs. | The generic claim that pangenomic calls add expression-association value is already directly tested. Kinnex and ONT direct RNA differ, and this does not establish that Lu tested the exact inserted-sequence splice endpoint proposed below. |

The Lu report is a preprint. A fresh bioRxiv publication-linkage query returned no linked publication on 2026-11-05 UTC; an empty linkage response is not proof that none exists. [Existing MEI work](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-020-02101-4) also studies insertion-associated expression and splicing, while the project's [prior-art check](2026-11-04-mei-prior-art-check.md) identifies clinical examples using insertion sequence and SpliceAI. The search here is bounded, not a claim of field-wide novelty or exhaustive coverage.

The only plausible residual worth a small falsifier is **an exact, allele-linked splice junction involving a resolved non-reference insertion that a fair existing sequence-aware and linked-small-variant baseline fails to explain**. A different RNA platform, more named samples, a stronger p-value, or rediscovery of a known locus is not enough. Even a verified molecular difference would not by itself establish disease causation or clinical benefit.

## Ascertainment, identity and confounding

The [RNA manuscript](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-4613444/v2) reports that its 60 LCL donors were unrelated and of European ancestry. I did not independently recheck pedigree, genotype identity, cell-line passage, or material provenance. The study's ancestry and transformed-cell context limit generalization; a shared title is not proof of the same aliquot, absence of cell-line-specific changes, or adequate per-locus coverage.

The manuscript says FLAIR's downstream analysis excluded 42,065 transcripts that did not align with a GENCODE v46 gene, among other support and expression filters. This does **not** mean that all novel isoforms were excluded, or establish which rows occur in the downloaded count file. It does mean that missing or filtered transcript entries cannot be called biological negatives. Bambu discovery used a multi-sample framework. Lu likewise describes an IsoQuant catalogue built jointly from Kinnex samples. Before claiming held-out discovery, establish exactly which donors contributed reads to the catalogue and freeze it using external or training donors only. A shared catalogue may support a labeled transductive analysis; it is not an independent test of discovery in unseen donors. The present header check does not establish the FLAIR catalogue's per-donor construction.

Use the allele and donor as separate units. Confirm the precise breakpoint and inserted sequence, genotype confidence, copy number, phase and reference assembly; unknown or ambiguous cases stay unknown. The RNA authors' methods use GRCh37 with lifted GENCODE v46, while the Vienna phased release has a CHM13/graph context and its header does not declare assembly. A coordinate-only lift-over can fail in repeats or change the represented insertion. The Zenodo inventory has no named BAM index; an index may exist elsewhere, but access to allele-informative molecules is unproved.

Compare the SV with linked SNPs and indels, local haplotype and copy number, population structure, expression depth, batch and cell-line effects. If an SV and SNP are nearly perfectly linked, conditioning cannot identify which causes the RNA pattern; report an unresolved equivalence class. Reference mapping, repetitive insertions, transcript assignment, poly(A) selection and truncated RNA molecules can generate apparent allelic differences. A count matrix is not proof that an inserted sequence and splice junction occur on the same molecule.

For scale, **if** all 56 were independent and a variant had allele frequency 1% under Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, the expected number of carriers would be only about 1.1; actual frequencies, genotypes and power are unknown. The mixed 58 cannot be substituted for a locus-specific callable sample count. A sample, read, transcript, and independent allele are different denominators.

## One cheap falsifier for the paired proposal

**Do not run this as part of the present review.** The following is a finite feasibility and baseline screen, not a powered association study. Its thresholds are operational stop rules, not proof of general absence.

1. Before examining RNA outcomes, freeze one endpoint: a non-reference insertion of at least 50 bp whose inserted sequence participates in an allele-linked splice junction. Verify the HGSVC2 release reference, and freeze an annotation version and a single test window, `chr1:1,000,001–2,000,000` on the verified GRCh38 representation. If the source cannot be shown to use that representation, stop rather than silently liftover. From the biallelic no-SNV VCF, select at most the first ten sequence-resolved insertion alleles in coordinate order with a breakpoint within 100 bp of a protein-coding exon boundary. Selection and exclusions use DNA and the frozen annotation only, never an RNA association or a favorable prior result.
2. Cap the initial indexed source check at 32 MiB of new transfer, including indexes and ranges. Record every candidate, unsupported representation, missing genotype, and reference mismatch. Require at least two distinct candidate alleles in different genes, each with two independently identified carriers and two non-carriers with callable genotypes, and a documented way to obtain allele-informative RNA for the relevant donors. These are minimal feasibility gates, not a claim of statistical power. If the bounded sources cannot establish them, stop this packet as a route to the proposed study. Do not download full nominal-QTL files: the source inventories list multi-gigabyte files.
3. Only if those gates pass, freeze baseline predictions before examining RNA: the RNA authors' existing transcript/SNP analysis, linked SNP/indel and gene-level explanations, plus a current sequence-aware splice/annotation method given the **same full insertion and local haplotype**. Record unsupported inputs separately; lack of tool output is not an automatic method win. Use previously published examples, such as HGSVC3's ZNF718, only as development controls, never as blinded test cases.
4. For a later separately specified, low-cost read check, require molecule-level evidence of the inserted sequence and exact junction, robust mapping and phase, plus non-carrier controls. A shared pooled discovery catalogue is not a held-out truth set. If all adequately evaluable cases are explained by the frozen baseline, stop this proposal. If all are unobservable or ambiguous, stop for lack of feasibility; do not call that a negative biological result. A single unexplained case permits a new independent, gene/allele-held-out protocol; it is not a major positive result or permission for an expensive campaign.

## TRACERx: conditional assessment

[Abbosh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05776-4) report patient-specific anchored-multiplex PCR panels tracking a median of 200 tumor mutations in 1,069 plasma samples from 197 TRACERx patients, with ECLIPSE estimating subclonal composition. Tumor tissue exomes and targeted plasma data are different assays. The selected mutation-panel reads cannot be assumed to span arbitrary SV junctions. A finding of no junction reads requires an authorized assay/read-level check; it cannot be inferred just from a public metadata page or equated with absence of the clone.

The closer [Williams et al., 2025 CloneSeq-SV study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09580-0) combines single-cell tumor WGS, clone-specific SV/SNV capture and serial cfDNA in advanced ovarian cancer. The authors report clone-frequency estimates from both SVs and SNVs and a subset comparison against cfDNA WGS; this is direct prior art for clone-level SV/SNV tracking, not merely ctDNA positivity. Its ovarian setting and assay differ from early-stage NSCLC and ECLIPSE. Those differences leave a **question**, not proof of incremental value. The paper's comparator uses SV raw reads and SNV duplex consensus, so assay and analysis conditions must be preserved when interpreting comparisons. Eleven of 12 cfDNA-WGS samples agreeing with the dominant SV clone are repeated samples from six patients, not 12 independent patients or independent proof of all relapse-seeding clones.

The Williams paper says supplementary tables contain patient-level SV and SNV clone frequencies, coverage, tumor fractions and error rates; it points to controlled raw data at `phs002857.v3.p1` and processed [Synapse project](https://www.synapse.org/Synapse:syn66399325). Anonymous project metadata listed a README and a processed-data archive, but individual file-handle metadata returned HTTP 403; Synapse contents, sizes and terms were not verified. Following the earlier [bounded supplement inventory](2026-11-06-cloneseq-supplement-inventory.md), a separate [workbook metadata check](../../results/cloneseq_workbook_metadata_20261106_run01/metadata.json) recorded 14 sheets and inspected their legends. I independently rehashed the 18,466,636-byte public archive and verified both member CRCs; its MD5 matched the recorded publisher value. The legends describe per-sample coverage and tumor fractions, SV and SNV clone frequencies, error rates, individual marker read counts and metadata, and clinical/recurrence annotations. No worksheet patient rows were inspected.

The public tables **may** support a cheap content-feasibility and falsification protocol. The metadata and legends do not yet establish usable patient/clone/time joins, denominators, assay failures, independently held-out relapse labels, matched budgets, or the inputs needed to run ECLIPSE. Reusing the same patients, clone catalogue and outcomes used to develop CloneSeq-SV would be an in-sample reanalysis. A genuine test needs a prespecified patient-disjoint split or independent cohort and access to the required raw or sufficiently detailed inputs.

The [EGA WGS dataset](https://ega-archive.org/datasets/EGAD50000001693) describes tumor/normal WGS for 260 TRACERx patients and serial blood collection. The [multiregion exome dataset](https://ega-archive.org/datasets/EGAD00001009825) lists 2,193 samples in the 421-patient cohort. Both are controlled. These counts, the 197-person ctDNA analysis, and the existence of longitudinal blood do not establish their patient-level intersection, available relapse tissue, clonally informative SVs, or permission to use stored plasma. A WGS tumor/normal pair alone does not provide a multiregion clone tree. Do not add patient, tissue and plasma counts or treat timepoints as independent patients.

The scout's proposed source-only gate is useful for public access terms, assay design and reported dataset descriptions, but **checking whether plasma reads span a specified junction is not source-only**. It requires an approved read-level query, or a separate validated assay on permitted stored material. First obtain an authorized, deidentified availability table linking, per patient, primary multiregion tissue and WGS, panel/bait design, serial plasma and usable DNA amount, relapse-site tissue with collection time, outcomes, and access/consent. If that table or the required material is unavailable, stop for feasibility, not a biological null. No contact or access request was made in this review.

If a real join exists, freeze the primary clone map and marker-selection rules before looking at relapse, and blind the plasma comparison to relapse tissue. Use independent relapse-site molecular evidence where available, allowing unknown or multiple seeding clones and incomplete sampling; do not manufacture a perfect ground truth from the same markers used by both methods. Compare the published mutation-panel/ECLIPSE baseline with SV-only and combined approaches on the **same patients and timepoints**, matched for plasma input, molecule counts, assay error and total sequencing/design cost. Prespecify patient-level assignment accuracy, abstentions, false-positive rate, lead time and censoring; account for repeated plasma measurements. Report clone-specific marker scarcity, loss of markers, copy number and assay dropout. A more sensitive ctDNA call alone does not establish more accurate clone assignment, treatment benefit or survival improvement.
