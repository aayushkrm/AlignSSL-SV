# Filtering prior art: full main-article methods

Date: 7 October 2026. The full publication objective was read before retrieval. This sidecar constrains prior-art claims; it does not select a research direction or approve an experiment.

**Finding:** both main articles describe empirically evaluated SV scoring/filtering. Neither full main article establishes a validated bound on error among retained calls under incomplete truth or assay/depth transfer. Model probabilities, adaptive filtering, and the word “calibration” are not themselves such a bound. This conclusion covers the main articles read here; supplementary contents and code remain unread. [ContextSV](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/), [Dual-SVF](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13502093/).

## Methods and evidence

| Check | ContextSV / ContextScore | Dual-SVF |
|---|---|---|
| **Prediction target** | ContextSV integrates CIGAR/split-read breakpoints, DBSCAN, and a PennCNV-Seq-derived HMM using coverage log2 ratio, SNV B-allele frequency, and population frequency. ContextScore is a **binary TP/FP classifier of ContextSV calls**, not a paralog-specific allele-dosage model. It uses 26 caller/context features, including support, breakpoint depth, HMM likelihood, local SV density, and segmental-duplication/repeat annotations. [Implementation and scorer methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/#SEC2). | Candidate-window refinement with sequence features encoded by ResNet-50 and CIGAR topology by MobileNetV2. Genomic priors guide sigmoid modality weights, fusion, contrastive learning, and cross-modal consistency. The primary classifier predicts **NEG, INS, or DEL** and assigns the maximum-probability class. This is candidate classification, not genotype dosage or a prediction set. [Full methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13502093/). |
| **Labels** | TP/FP calls come from HG002, the Platinum Pedigree trio, and simulated long-read benchmarks; benchmark evaluation uses Truvari 5.3.0. The pooled scorer comparison contains 86,846 TP and 62,311 FP. HG002 truth is GIAB SV v0.6 Tier 1; pedigree truth is a merged multi-caller/assembly callset; simulated truth comprises VISOR-inserted gnomAD-derived variants. These are heterogeneous operational labels, not uniform independent adjudication of every call. [Scorer methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/#SEC2-4), [benchmark results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/#SEC3). | Main text defines VCF INS/DEL records as positives and uses NIRBS to generate nonoverlapping reference intervals as negatives, with lengths matched to positives under a Poisson-like sampling prior. It asserts that sampled negatives include difficult contexts. The main article does **not specify which exact positive-label VCF/training cohort supplies all positives or orthogonal verification of every NEG interval**; details may be in the unread supplement/code. [Training sample generation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13502093/). |
| **Donor / locus / platform splits** | Three leave-one-dataset-out models exclude, respectively, all HG002, the simulated dataset, or the Platinum Pedigree group. A final pooled model is used for deployment. Separately: stratified 80/20 classification comparison and leave-one-chromosome-out validation. Main text does not specify disjoint loci across dataset groups. Training-source reads are HG002 ONT R10.4.1, pedigree ONT ultra-long, and ONT simulation. PacBio HiFi HG008 is outside the stated training pool, but its evaluation is tumor-minus-normal somatic calling; it is not target-assay risk calibration. Exact scorer-model assignment to HG008/KOLF is not separately detailed. [Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/#SEC2-4), [HG008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/#SEC3-9). | Main text lists simulated human/plant/yeast ONT/CLR data; real HG002 CLR 69→35→20→10→5×, ONT 48→20→10→5×, and CCS 28→10→5×; CHM13; HG002–004 and HG005–007 trios; and real plant/yeast datasets. **Training/validation/test fractions and exclusions by donor, locus/chromosome, or platform are not stated in the full main article.** Multiple evaluation platforms do not establish an untouched platform holdout without that information. Supplement/code may resolve it. [Full methods/results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13502093/). |
| **Model and threshold selection** | Logistic regression, RF, SVM, and XGBoost are compared. Hyperparameters use stratified CV optimizing precision, with class balancing and length-bin weighting. RF reports highest test AUC (0.968; XGBoost 0.960). For each sample/SV type, a two-component Gaussian mixture sets a probability cutoff. Fallback **0.2** if fewer than 20 calls or weak fit: BIC gain <10, component gap <0.08, separation <1.0, or smaller component weight <0.10. Cutoff is constrained to score percentiles 5–85 and capped at **0.3**. These are distribution-dependent filtering rules, not a preset error target. [Scorer methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/#SEC2-4). | Sigmoid confidence gates weight internal modalities; the posterior class output is filtered through **argmax**, with NEG candidates removed. MAC combines representation learning, prior-weighted contrastive loss, and JS-divergence consistency regularization. Main text calls the learned boundary dynamically calibrated, but gives no numerical post-hoc risk cutoff, complete hyperparameter/model-selection protocol, or independently reserved probability-calibration set. These details could exist in unread material. [Full methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13502093/). |
| **Probability / risk / abstention meaning** | RF probabilities and HMM likelihoods are used as scores. GMM thresholds adapt filtering to a sample's score distribution. The HMM can return **unknown/no consensus**; no controlled error guarantee is attached to that outcome. Full main text reports AUC, F1, precision/recall, and chromosome CV, but no calibrated probability-versus-outcome study or nominal selective error/coverage bound under incomplete labels or transfer. [Scorer methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/#SEC2-4), [discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/#SEC4). | “Calibration” refers to feature alignment, modality balance, adaptive decision boundaries, and MAC training. Main results evaluate AUC/F1, FP suppression, depth/platform performance, and Mendelian discordance. NEG is a predicted class, not an uncertainty refusal policy with a stated risk target. Full main text does not establish empirical probability calibration or a selective error/coverage guarantee. This is a main-article finding only. [Full methods/results/discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13502093/). |
| **Known limitations / strength of validation** | Authors explicitly acknowledge elevated FP burden, few >100 kb training labels, and potential generalization failure with different SV distributions, depth, and error profiles. They call for stronger independent benchmark/platform holdouts. Complex ploidy/mapping-ambiguous regions are not separately handled; representations assume single events and do not support translocations/nested complex events. KOLF validation is **two selected deletions** by breakpoint PCR/Sanger, not validation of all scored calls. Their child-unique “Mendelian error” metric is affected by parental FNs/child FPs and is not direct full genotype truth. [Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/#SEC4), [KOLF](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/#SEC3-11), [pedigree metric](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/#SEC3-10). | Authors identify localized receptive-field limits for inversions and translocations. Main classifier remains NEG/INS/DEL; additional DUP/INV/TRS analysis is referenced to supplement §4.3. CHM13 self-reference assumes an ideal zero-SV control; other truth uses Dipcall assembly calls, simulation, and pedigree consistency. Main acknowledges nonhuman datasets were curated for pangenome/population analysis rather than precise individual-call truth. These controls do not establish a bound for all difficult unlabeled calls. [Full results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13502093/). |

## Narrow prior-art constraints

Treat ContextScore as close prior art for caller/context features, classifier probabilities, dataset/chromosome holdouts, and adaptive per-sample filtering. Treat Dual-SVF as close prior art for sequence-plus-alignment fusion, genomic priors, and filtering evaluated across depth/platform/species. Merely adding those features, dynamic thresholds, or more depth curves would have substantial overlap. [ContextSV](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13554283/), [Dual-SVF](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13502093/).

A distinct risk-control claim would need an explicit accepted-call error target, independent calibration/test units, and assumptions for missing truth and transfer. AUC/F1 improvements, learned gates, score-distribution thresholds, and Mendelian consistency do not by themselves establish that claim. This is a methodological inference from the verified main articles, not evidence that supplementary material, code, or other literature lacks relevant analyses.
